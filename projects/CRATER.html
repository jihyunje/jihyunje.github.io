CRATER

Robotics team project · 2022–2025 · Student Team Lead

CRATER is a Caltech student rover team building a rugged, autonomous field robot for terrain traversal, manipulation, and search-and-rescue tasks at URC and CIRC.

Gallery

Project details

Overview

CRATER was founded in September 2022 by Caltech undergraduates with the shared goal of developing a rover capable of operating in remote environments. The team iterated through multiple chassis and arm designs and prepared for competitions including the Canadian International Rover Challenge (CIRC) and the University Rover Challenge (URC), where the rover must complete tasks involving mobility, manipulation, autonomy, and payload handling.

My contribution

As student team lead, I helped guide the rover’s systems development and competition strategy. My work centered on team coordination and technical direction across the rover’s chassis, arm, power, communication, and mission readiness efforts as the project moved from development into field validation.

Tools and methods

CRATER uses a rocker-bogie suspension for rough-terrain stability, a high-torque drivetrain for mobility, and a 6-DOF robotic arm designed to lift payloads and perform manipulation tasks. The system integrates ROS2-based motion control, a modular electrical stack, wireless communication links, and field testing in mission-like terrain and competition conditions.
